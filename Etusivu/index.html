There's actually quite a few terms...
  • Autolyse = Technique where water and flour are mixed together and allowed to rest before adding other ingredients. This way the dough becomes smoother and requires less mechanical kneading.
  • Proofing = The stage where bread rises. Bread can under-proof or over-proof based on time. In under-proofing bread hasn't gotten enough fermentation time, while in over-proofing it has gotten too much time.
  • Scoring = Process where one makes shallow cuts in proofed dough before baking. It prevents random tearing, but it is also used as a way to stylize bread.
  • Ear = Raised flap of crust, often seen in sourdough breads.
  • Oven spring = Quick rise and expansion of dough during the first 10-15 minutes of baking, before the crust hardens. Can increase the volume of bread by up to 30%
  • Baker's percentage (or Baker's Math) = Perhaps not so common term, but still interesting one in my opinion. It's a method of expressing ingredient proportions in baking, all ingredients are expressed as a percentage of the total flour weight:

E.g., 1000g flour = 100%. 700g water = 70%. Because 700:1000 = 0.7 = 70%. In baker's math the percentage always goes over 100%

Answer
  1. Measuring ingredients is the most important thing when baking.
    1. Too much flour makes the bread dry.
    2. Too much yeast causes dough to rise too quickly.
    3. Too much water causes the bread to flatten.
  2. Timing is also important. Besides the cooking time, allowing the bread to rise is important.
    1. Sourdoughs are usually slightly underproofed.
    2. Focaccia doesn't care about longer proofing.

  • Q: Why is my bread hollow inside?

    A: Most likely the bread is under-proofed. This can lead to a dense and hollow interior.

  • Q: Why is my bread flat?

    A: This can happen if the bread is over-proofed.

  • Q: Why is my bread gummy?

    A: The bread is most likely under-baked, underfermented or there's too much moisture in the dough.

  • Q: Why is my bread hard after baking?

    A: The bread is probably over-baked or the oven temperature was too high.

  • Q: Why didn't my dough rise?

    A: The most common causes for this are expired or dead yeast, and liquid that is too cold or hot.

  • Q: Why didn't my dough stretch?

    A: The lack of stretching can be caused by low hydration, over-kneading, wrong flour type or inadequate resting time.

  • Q: Why didn't my dough brown?

    A: The dough fails to brown if the oven temperature is too low, or the dough is either over-proofed or under-proofed.
